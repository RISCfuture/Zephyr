If the same file was changed in two places

When a file is changed in two places before either has synced, Dropbox keeps both rather than choosing, and the second one turns up beside the first under a name Dropbox gave it.

What you’ll see

  • Two files in the folder: the one you know, holding whichever change reached Dropbox first, and a second file named after it.

  • Dropbox chooses that name, not Zephyr. It says which account and which day the copy came from.

  • Nothing in Sync Issues, and no notification of a failure. Nothing failed: Zephyr never writes over a change it didn’t know about, so keeping both is the answer.

Both files are ordinary files in your Dropbox from that moment on, on this Mac and on every device signed in to the account.

Decide which one to keep

  1. Open both and compare them.

  2. Copy anything you want out of the one you’re not keeping.

  3. Delete the one you’re not keeping, or rename it if you want to hold on to it.

    Deleting removes it from Dropbox and from your other devices too — see If you deleted something you needed.

Note: The file you kept still has its own history, so a change you meant to keep and then wrote over is recoverable on its own — see Go back to an earlier version of a file.

Make it happen less

  • Let Zephyr run. Finder only hears about a change made elsewhere while Zephyr is running, and a Mac that hasn’t heard is a Mac about to make a second copy — see Keep Zephyr running in the background.

  • Give a Mac that has been asleep or offline a moment to catch up before editing anything in your Dropbox. The menu-bar panel says when it is done.

  • Watch for this after a spell with syncing paused, which is exactly the situation that produces it — see Pause syncing.
