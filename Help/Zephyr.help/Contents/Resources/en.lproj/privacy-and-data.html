What Zephyr sends, and what it doesn’t

Zephyr moves your files between your Mac and Dropbox. That is almost the whole of what it sends anywhere, and there is no Zephyr server for any of it to go to. The rest is a crash report and an update check, both described below.

Your files

File contents travel directly between your Mac and Dropbox over encrypted connections, and pass through nothing in between. Zephyr downloads a file when something opens it, and uploads one when you create or change it.

Once your files are with Dropbox, Dropbox’s own privacy policy governs what happens to them — worth reading alongside this.

Your Dropbox credentials

You type your Dropbox password on Dropbox’s website, never in Zephyr. What Dropbox hands back is a refresh token, which Zephyr keeps in the macOS Keychain and sends only to Dropbox.

Zephyr asks Dropbox for six kinds of access and no others: reading your account’s basic profile and usage; reading the names, sizes, and timestamps of your files; downloading file contents; creating and changing files; listing shared links; and creating and revoking them. Dropbox shows you the list when you link the account. Zephyr asks for no team, contacts, or Paper access, and can see no account other than the one you link.

You can withdraw access at any time, from Dropbox’s own connected-apps settings or by unlinking the account in Zephyr.

The index on your Mac

So Finder can list your Dropbox without downloading it, Zephyr keeps a database describing your account: each item’s name, path, size, timestamps, content hash, and whether you excluded it, plus about a week of recent sync history and notes about anything that failed.

It never leaves your Mac. It is not uploaded anywhere and not readable by anyone else. Unlinking the account removes it.

Crash reports

When Zephyr stops working — the app, the part of it the Finder runs, the window that lists a file’s earlier versions, the Share extension, the widget, or the zephyr command — it reports the fault to Sentry, an error-monitoring service, so it can be found and fixed. A report says which of those was running, what version it was, what kind of Mac it was on, and where in Zephyr’s own code things went wrong. From the app and the part the Finder runs, it also sends a sample of how long Zephyr’s own work took, so slow spots can be found.

It carries nothing of yours. File names, folder names, and Dropbox paths are stripped out of every message in the report before it is sent, as are email addresses and anything resembling a credential. Zephyr also tells Sentry not to attach your IP address or your Mac’s name, and there is no Zephyr account for a report to be attached to. What ties one report to another is a random number Zephyr made up on this Mac; removing Zephyr’s data throws it away.

There is no setting for this yet. Reports from development builds are never sent at all.

Checking for updates

The downloadable version checks GitHub for new releases. The request is unauthenticated and says nothing about you, your account, or your files. Set Check automatically to Never in Zephyr’s settings to stop it — see Check for Zephyr updates. The App Store version doesn’t make it at all.

There is no advertising identifier, no cross-app tracking, and nothing sold or shared with anyone.

Diagnostic logging

Zephyr writes messages to the macOS unified log. They stay on your Mac; nothing from them is uploaded, to Sentry or anywhere else.

Note: Unified log data is included in a sysdiagnose. If you generate one and send it to Apple, or attach one to a bug report, that log data goes where you send it. Look before you share. The same goes for a diagnostic report — see Collect a diagnostic report.

Read the full policy

The policy is at riscfuture.github.io/Zephyr/privacy.html. Zephyr links it too: click Zephyr in the menu bar, click Settings…, then click Privacy Policy under About.
